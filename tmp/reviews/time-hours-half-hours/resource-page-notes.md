# Time, Hours and Half Hours: Resource Page Notes

Grade 1 Math. Five printable worksheets that teach children to
read and show times on an analog clock, in hours and half hours,
and to match those times to digital times. Only hours and half
hours appear: no quarter hours, no minutes by fives, no AM/PM,
and no elapsed time.

## What children learn

- The parts of an analog clock: the face, the 12 numbers around
  it, the short hour hand, the long minute hand, and the dot in
  the center.
- The short hand names the hour. The long hand tells o'clock or
  half past.
- At o'clock, the long hand points to the 12 and the short hand
  points to the hour number.
- At half past, the long hand points to the 6 and the short hand
  sits halfway between two hour numbers, because half the hour
  has passed. It never sits on the hour number at half past.
- The same time can be shown on an analog clock and written as
  a digital time (3:00, 7:30).
- At 12:00 the two hands overlap. The short hand shows as the
  thicker base of the shared line.

## Page by page

### Page 1: Meet the Clock

A big labeled clock shows the face, the 12 numbers, the short
hour hand, the long minute hand, and the center dot. Children
color the short hand teal and the long hand coral on three
clocks, then circle the short hour hand on each of two check
clocks.

Answers: coloring clocks: short hands point to the 4, between
the 10 and the 11, and to the 1; long hands point to the 12,
the 6, and the 12. Check clocks: the short hour hands point to
the 8 and between the 9 and the 10.

### Page 2: O'Clock Hours

A worked example shows 3:00 with the words "3 o'clock": the long
hand points to 12, the short hand points to the hour. Children
write the digital time under each of four clocks.

Answers: top row 5:00 and 9:00; bottom row 12:00 and 7:00.

### Page 3: Half Past

A worked example shows 7:30 with the words "half past 7": the
long hand points to 6, the short hand sits between the 7 and
the 8. Children draw a line from each analog clock to its
digital time.

Answers: top left clock to 4:30, top right clock to 8:30,
bottom left clock to 11:30, bottom right clock to 2:30. 9:00 is
the spare digital time and matches no clock.

### Page 4: Show the Time

A worked example shows 6:00 with its hands drawn in. Children
draw both hands on four blank numbered faces from the digital
time given under each.

Answers: 4:00: short hand on the 4, long hand on the 12. 10:00:
short hand on the 10, long hand on the 12. 1:30: short hand
halfway between the 1 and the 2, long hand on the 6. 9:30:
short hand halfway between the 9 and the 10, long hand on the 6.

### Page 5: Find the Clock

Three questions, each giving one digital time above three analog
clocks. Children circle the clock that shows it. Each set's
wrong clocks differ in exactly one hand, so both hands must be
checked, and the correct clock sits in a different position
each time.

Answers: 8:00 is the middle clock (8:30 has the long hand on
the 6; 9:00 has the short hand on the 9). 5:30 is the right
clock (5:00 has the long hand on the 12; 6:30 has the short hand
between the 6 and the 7). 12:30 is the left clock (1:00 has the
short hand on the 1; 1:30 has the short hand between the 1 and
the 2).

## Tips for grown-ups

- Ask "How do you know?" and let your child point to each hand.
  The short hand names the hour. The long hand tells o'clock or
  half past.
- The most common half past mistake is reading the hour from
  the nearer number. At 7:30 the hour is 7, the hour just
  passed, even though the short hand leans toward the 8.
- Practice on a real clock at home. Point to the hands at
  breakfast or bedtime and ask what time it shows.
- This pack carries no separate answer key, by design. The
  answers above live in these resource notes only.
