# Thanksgiving Fun & Learning Pack (Preschool & Kindergarten)

A cheerful Thanksgiving pack for preschool and kindergarten. Eight
printable pages plus a separate parent answer key, all with warm
autumn pictures, large friendly illustrations, and one clear task on
every page.

## What children learn

- Say and write what they are thankful for, in words or drawings.
- Cut out pieces and build a turkey by following a model.
- Recognize numbers 1 to 5 and match each number to a color.
- Form lowercase letters on primary handwriting guidelines and match
  words to pictures.
- Count objects by kind in a grid and write each total.
- Finish AB, AAB, and ABC repeating patterns.
- Put four pumpkin-growing stages in order.
- Search a busy scene and count each kind of object.

## The eight pages

1. I Am Thankful For...: draw or write 5 things the child is thankful
   for, one in each numbered space. Drawing counts as a full answer.
2. Build a Turkey: cut out 7 pieces (feather fan, head, body, two
   wings, two feet) and glue them in the work area to build one turkey,
   copying the small model picture.
3. Turkey Color by Number: color one large turkey using the legend.
   1 brown, 2 dark brown, 3 light tan, 4 red, 5 yellow.
4. Thanksgiving Words: Trace and Match: trace turkey, pumpkin, apple,
   leaf, corn, and pie, then circle the picture that matches each word.
5. Count the Thanksgiving Pictures: count each kind of picture in a
   mixed grid and write each total in the recording table.
6. Finish the Feather Pattern: color the white feathers to finish AB,
   AAB, and ABC patterns. A small color key shows red, orange, and
   yellow.
7. Pumpkin Growing Sequence: number the 4 pictures in growing order,
   1 to 4: seed, sprout, growing plant, pumpkin.
8. Thanksgiving I Spy: search the autumn scene and count pumpkins,
   leaves, apples, acorns, and turkeys.

## Parent answer key

A separate one-page parent answer key covers Pages 3, 5, 6, 7, and 8.
It carries only the page titles, the correct answers, and one line of
brief parent guidance per page. It sits beside the student pages,
never inside them. Pages 1, 2, and 4 need no key: Page 1 is the
child's own answers, Page 2 has the model picture on the page, and
Page 4 answers are the words themselves.

## Parent guidance

- Pages 1 and 7 work with talking first. Ask the question aloud, let
  your child answer in words or a drawing, then write. Drawing counts
  as a full answer on Page 1.
- Page 2 needs scissors and glue. An adult should be nearby for
  cutting, and the small finished turkey in the corner shows where each
  piece goes.
- Pages 3 and 6 use color as part of the task, so they print best in
  color. For a black and white print of Page 6, write the color word
  inside each colored feather first.
- Pages 5 and 8 both practice counting, in two different ways: a tidy
  grid first, then a busy scene. Encourage your child to mark or point
  to each picture as they count it.

## Grade 1 extensions

Each page has a short extension for first graders. These live on the
resource page, not on the child pages.

- Page 1: write a full sentence for at least 2 rows, starting with
  "I am thankful for", and read one sentence aloud.
- Page 2: count the lobes on the feather fan and write the number on
  the body, then write the color word for one lobe on its back before
  gluing.
- Page 3: count and write how many sections use each number (1 appears
  3 times, 2 appears 5 times, 3 appears 5 times, 4 appears 2 times,
  5 appears 1 time).
- Page 4: after tracing, write each word once from memory on the blank
  guideline under it.
- Page 5: answer two questions under the table: "Which picture has the
  most?" (leaf) and "How many pumpkins and apples in all?" (5 + 4 = 9).
- Page 6: start a new row: the child makes their own ABC pattern with
  3 colors and a partner finishes it.
- Page 7: write or dictate one sentence for each stage ("First a
  seed...") and add a fifth box: what happens next (picking the
  pumpkin).
- Page 8: write one addition sentence from the scene, for example
  pumpkins + acorns = 4 + 5 = 9, and find which object appears exactly
  one more time than the apples (pumpkins, 4).

## Standards note

The activities line up with common early-learning expectations for
this age group: counting and writing numbers to 10 and beyond,
recognizing numbers 1 to 5, sorting and counting objects by kind,
building shapes and pictures from parts, forming letters on
guidelines, retelling a simple sequence in order, and repeating
patterns, which are a foundation for later algebra thinking. This is a
general alignment note only. It is not an official endorsement by any
standards body, and no standards codes appear on the child pages or
on the answer key.
