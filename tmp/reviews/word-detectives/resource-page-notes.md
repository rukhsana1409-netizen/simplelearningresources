## How to use this pack

This pack is about reading printed letters and words. Each digraph is
two letters that make one sound: sh, ch, th, wh, ph. All words the
child reads are one syllable with short vowels, plus blends the child
already knows. Say the sound, not the letter names: "sh says sh", not
"s-h". If the child is stuck on a word, cover the digraph with a
finger, read the rest of the word, then add the digraph sound at the
start or the end. One page a day; praise the detective work.

The th in thumb, thin, and bath is the quiet th sound. The th in the,
this, and that is the buzzy th sound; those words are learned whole as
high-frequency words, not sounded out here. Ph says f, like fish.
Graph and Phil are the words to read; phone is the meet example on
Page 1. Wh says w; whale is the meet example on Page 1, and its
long-a spelling comes in Resource 3, Long Vowels. The ck endings in
chick, thick, and whack were met in Kindergarten (duck in My First
Reading Stories).

## Page 1: Meet the Digraphs (RF.1.3a)

The adult models each digraph sound first, then reads the example
word aloud. The child circles the two letters in each word that make
one sound. Sounds: sh says sh, ch says ch, th says th (quiet), wh
says w, ph says f. Example words: ship, chick, thumb, whale, phone.
Whale and phone are meet examples only, read by the adult; their
long-vowel spellings are not taught yet.

## Page 2: Read and Match: Digraphs at the Start (RF.1.3a, b)

The child reads the word and draws a line to the picture it names.
Answers, in order: ship, shell, chick, chop, thumb, whip. Pictures
on the right are in mixed order, so no picture sits beside its own
word.

## Page 3: Find the Sound at the End (RF.1.3a, b)

The child reads the word, then circles the two letters at the end
that make one sound. The picture beside each word checks the meaning.
Answers, in order: fish (sh), dish (sh), bench (ch), lunch (ch),
bath (th), moth (th).

## Page 4: Sort the Words (RF.1.3a, b)

The child reads each word in the bank and writes it in the column of
its digraph. Phil is a person's name, so it starts with a capital P.
No other untaught vowel pattern is introduced in this pack. Answers:
sh: shop, shut. ch: chip, chop. th: thin, thick. wh: whip, whack.
ph: graph, Phil. Detective check at the bottom: the child reads the
sorted words back and checks each word has its digraph.

## Page 5: Read the Sentences (RF.1.3b; RF.1.1a light touch)

The child reads the sentence and draws a line to the picture it tells
about. Every word is decodable from taught patterns or a
high-frequency word (the, is, in, can, on, has, a). The adult points
out the capital letter at the start and the period at the end.
Answers, in order:
1. "The fish is in the dish." (fish in a dish)
2. "The chick can sit on the bench." (chick on a bench)
3. "The ship is on the sand." (ship on sand)
4. "The chick is in the shop." (chick inside a shop)
The two chick sentences use visually distinct pictures: chick on a
bench vs chick inside a shop between shelves.

## What is new against Kindergarten

Kindergarten teaches single letter sounds and CVC words only; no
digraph appears anywhere in Kindergarten. This pack adds the five
consonant digraphs as one sound each, reading one-syllable words with
a digraph at the start or the end, sorting words by digraph, and
reading short decodable sentences. It does not repeat Resource 1,
Sounds in Words, which was phonemic awareness by ear with no printed
target words. No long vowels, final-e, or vowel teams (Resource 3);
no two-syllable words (Resource 10).
