# Addition Fact Strategies Within 20 — Resource Page Notes

Grade 1 Math. Five printable worksheets that teach children to add
within 20 by choosing a strategy, not by memorizing facts in isolation.

## What children learn

- Count on from the bigger number instead of starting at 1.
- Make 10 first: move just enough dots to fill a ten, then add what
  is left.
- Doubles are facts worth knowing by heart (6 + 6, 7 + 7, 8 + 8).
- Near doubles use a known double: one more, or one less.
- Different problems suit different strategies, and any strategy
  that gives the correct sum is right.

## Page by page

### Page 1: Count On from the Big Number

Children follow one worked number-line example (8 + 3 = 11: start at
8, hop to 9, 10, 11), then draw their own hops for three more
problems, always starting from the bigger number, which is already
circled.

Answers: 8 + 3 = 11; 4 + 9 = 13; 7 + 2 = 9; 5 + 9 = 14.

### Page 2: Make 10 First

A worked example shows the whole move: before, a frame with 9 dots
and one empty space beside a frame with 1 gold dot and 3 coral dots;
after, a filled ten-frame (9 teal dots plus the gold dot) beside the
3 remaining coral dots. The equation reads
9 + 4 = 9 + 1 + 3 = 10 + 3 = 13. Three practice problems follow the
same pattern with dashed empty slots and a small arrow showing the
dots that move.

Answers: 9 + 4 = 13; 8 + 6 = 14; 7 + 5 = 12; 9 + 7 = 16.

### Page 3: Doubles

A worked example (6 + 6 = 12) leads into matching three doubles
cards to their sums, finishing two doubles, and finding two missing
addends.

Answers: 7 + 7 goes to 14, 4 + 4 goes to 8, 5 + 5 goes to 10;
3 + 3 = 6, 8 + 8 = 16; 6 + 6 = 12, 7 + 7 = 14.

### Page 4: Near Doubles

A worked example (7 + 8 = 7 + 7 + 1 = 15) shows how a near double
borrows a known double. The extra dot is ringed inside its group
for "one more"; the removed dot is crossed out inside its group for
"one less", so both read clearly even in grayscale.

Answers: 7 + 8 = 15; 6 + 7 = 13; 5 + 6 = 11; 8 + 7 = 15;
9 + 8 = 17.

### Page 5: Pick Your Strategy

Six problems. For each, the child circles one of four strategy
choices (Count On, Make 10, Double, Near Double) and solves. The
most natural fit for each problem is: 9 + 2 Count On, 7 + 7 Double,
6 + 7 Near Double, 9 + 6 Make 10, 3 + 8 Count On, 8 + 5 Make 10.

Answers: 9 + 2 = 11; 7 + 7 = 14; 6 + 7 = 13; 9 + 6 = 15;
3 + 8 = 11; 8 + 5 = 13.

## Tips for grown-ups

- On page 5, praise the strategy the child picked when the sum is
  right. The most natural fit is listed, not the only one.
- If a child counts on their fingers, that is fine: counting on is
  one of the strategies this pack teaches.
- The separate parent answer key lists every answer. Nothing on the
  child pages names a strategy as the only correct one.
