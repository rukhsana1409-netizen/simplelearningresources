## How it goes beyond Kindergarten read-alouds

Kindergarten Story Retelling and Story Elements are adult read-alouds: the child answers from pictures, retells orally, orders pictures, and draws. Here the child reads connected Grade 1 text, answers WH questions about details in the text, orders written story parts, names the central message, and writes a retelling in sentences. Every story is decodable from taught patterns only (CVC words, blends, digraphs sh/ch/th/wh/ph, final-e, vowel teams ai/ay/ee/ea/oa/ow) plus high-frequency words. Every answer is findable in the story or its pictures. No prediction, no inference beyond the text, no two-syllable words, no untaught patterns.

## Page-by-page answers

### Page 1. Read and Find (RL.1.1, RL.1.7)
Story: "Sam has a cat. The cat is black. Sam and the cat sit in the sun. The cat can nap."
- Who is in the story? Sam (the boy).
- Where do they sit? In the sun.
- What does the cat do? Nap.

### Page 2. Who, What, Where? (RL.1.1, RL.1.3)
Story: "Ben has a red bike. The bike is big. Ben can ride the bike up the hill. At the top, Ben sees a red kite. The kite is in a tree."
Word bank: Ben, the hill, a red kite.
- Who is in the story? Ben.
- Where does Ben ride? The hill.
- What does Ben see? A red kite.

### Page 3. Put the Story in Order (RL.1.2)
Story: "Mia Bakes a Cake." The three panels are printed in mixed order; the child reads each part and writes 1, 2, or 3. Text-based ordering, not picture-only like Kindergarten.
- "Mia makes a big cake." is 2 (middle).
- "Mia and Sam eat the cake." is 3 (end).
- "Mia has eggs and milk." is 1 (beginning).
Phonics note: the panels were simplified to the stated scope. No "bowl" (ow before l) and no "-es" ending ("mixes"). Every word is CVC, final-e, ea, or a high-frequency word.

### Page 4. What Does the Story Teach? (RL.1.2)
Story: "Kim has a red kite. Kim sees Ben. Ben has no kite. Kim gives the kite to Ben. Ben is glad. Kim is glad."
- What does this story teach us? "It is kind to share."
- Finish the frame: "The story teaches us to share." Word bank: share, keep. Answer: share.
The lesson is in what Kim does and how they feel at the end.

### Page 5. Retell It Yourself (RL.1.2, RL.1.1)
Story: "Sam and Kim go to the lake. Sam has a big red mat. Kim has a box with cake. Sam sees a duck in the lake. The duck can swim. Sam and Kim watch the duck. Sam and Kim eat and play."
The children watch the duck swim while enjoying their picnic. Nobody feeds the duck.
Retell: Beginning (Sam and Kim go to the lake), Middle (they see the duck, it swims, they watch it), End (they eat and play). One sentence in each box. Word bank for spelling support: lake, duck, cake. Accept any retelling that keeps the three parts in order with the key details.

## Parent guidance

Say the story is for reading, not just looking: the child reads the words, the pictures help. If stuck on a word, cover the vowel team or the silent e, read the rest, then add the long sound. One page a day; reread the Page 5 story together before the retell.
