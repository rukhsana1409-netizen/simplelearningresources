## How to use this pack

This pack helps a child write a short how-to book about something
they know how to do, using one topic all the way through. Let the
child pick the topic. A familiar how-to works best: washing hands,
making a sandwich, planting a seed, or brushing teeth, or their own
idea. One page a day works well.

Page 1 picks the how-to: the child circles a picture or writes their
own idea, writes the book title, lists what they need, and draws the
finished result. Page 2 draws the steps in order: First, Next, Then,
Last. Page 3 writes the steps, one sentence per step, using the time
words, with a starter for the first two steps and open lines for the
last two. Page 4 closes the book with a closing line, adds one helpful
tip, and checks the book with a simple checklist.

## Standards (resource page only, never on the child pages)

W.1.2: write an informative piece that names a topic, supplies facts,
and closes the piece. W.1.8: recall information from experience to
answer a question.

## For adults

Let your child pick one how-to they know well, washing hands, making
a sandwich, planting a seed, or brushing teeth. Help them name the
steps in order, write each step with a time word, and add one true
fact or helpful tip. This pack builds two Grade 1 writing goals:
writing an informative piece that names a topic, supplies facts, and
closes the piece (W.1.2), and recalling information from experience
to answer a question (W.1.8). If your child cannot think of a how-to,
offer two familiar ones and let them choose one. Do not write the
steps for the child; the child's own words, even short ones, are the
goal.

## Page 1: Pick Your How-To

The child circles one picture or writes their own how-to idea, writes
the book title starting with "How to", lists what they need on three
handwriting rows, and draws what it looks like when they are done.

## Page 2: Order the Steps

Four large numbered drawing frames, First, Next, Then, Last, in a
2x2 grid. The child draws the four steps of their how-to in order,
one picture per frame.

## Page 3: Write the Steps

Four numbered writing panels matching Page 2 by number, word, and
colour. One spacious handwriting row per step. Steps 1 and 2 start
with "First, " and "Next, " printed on the line; steps 3 and 4 are
open lines where the child writes the full sentence with "Then" and
"Last".

## Page 4: Close It and Check It

The child writes a closing line starting with "Now you can", writes
one helpful tip in the lightbulb panel, and ticks a four-item
checklist: named the how-to, steps in order, included a true fact or
helpful tip, closed the book.
