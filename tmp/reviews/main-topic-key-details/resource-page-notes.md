# Main Topic and Key Details

## Resource details

- **Pack:** Main Topic and Key Details
- **Level:** Grade 1 Reading, Reading Comprehension
- **Subtitle:** Grade 1 Reading | Reading Comprehension
- **Standards:** RI.1.1 (ask and answer questions about key details in a text), RI.1.2 (identify the main topic and retell key details of a text). RI.1.3 (describe the connection between two ideas in a text) supports Page 4, and RI.1.7 (use illustrations to describe key ideas) supports the whole pack.
- **Pages:** 5, US Letter

## How it differs from the Kindergarten pack

The Kindergarten Informational Text pack (RI.K.1-3, RI.K.7) is adult read-aloud, and every child response is picture-based: circle the bee, the penguin, the cloud. This Grade 1 pack is a real step up. The child READS the passages themselves, answers in words and writing, and the work runs from supported (circle the main topic word) on Page 1 to independent (write the main topic and three facts, word bank only, no starters) on Page 5. The five topics are all new as passages and do not duplicate the Kindergarten topics (bees, penguins, rain, wheat to bread, teeth): bats, how a butterfly grows, your heart, the Moon, trains. The passages use familiar Grade 1 vocabulary and high-frequency words rather than staying tightly inside the phonics scope, at the user's direction, so they read as real nonfiction.

## Page-by-page answers

### Page 1. What Is This Text About? (RI.1.2, RI.1.1)
Passage: "Bats are animals. Bats can fly. Bats sleep in the day. Bats fly at night. Many bats eat bugs."
- What is this text about? Circle Bats. Every sentence tells about bats, so bats are the main topic. Night and bugs are in the text too, but they are only details, not the whole topic.
- When do bats fly? At night. Accept: in the night.
- What do many bats eat? Bugs. Accept: insects.
- When do bats sleep? In the day. Accept: during the day.
Word bank: night, day, bugs. The three written questions are the key addition: this page is a meaningful Grade 1 activity, not just one circle-the-answer question.

### Page 2. Find the Key Details (RI.1.1, RI.1.2)
Passage: "A butterfly starts as a small egg on a leaf. A caterpillar comes out of the egg. The caterpillar eats leaves and grows big. Then it makes a hard case. The case is called a chrysalis. A butterfly comes out of the case."
- What is this text about? A butterfly (how a butterfly grows).
- Circle three facts: the egg is on a leaf; the caterpillar eats leaves; a butterfly comes out of a chrysalis. Do NOT circle the caterpillar eating flowers or the butterfly sleeping in a nest; the text never says those things.

### Page 3. Ask and Answer (RI.1.1)
Passage: "Your heart is in your chest. It is about as big as your fist. Your heart pumps blood all around your body. Blood helps your body grow and stay strong. When you run and play, your heart beats fast. When you rest, your heart beats slow."
Word bank: chest, pumps blood, run and play, rest.
- Where is your heart? In your chest.
- What does your heart do? It pumps blood all around your body.
- When does your heart beat fast? When you run and play.

### Page 4. How Are These Facts Connected? (RI.1.3, RI.1.1)
Passage: "The Moon is in the sky at night. The Moon has no light of its own. The Sun lights up the Moon. That is why the Moon shines. The Moon moves around the Earth. As it moves, we see more of the bright part, or less of it. That is why the Moon looks different each night."
The science is simple and accurate: the Sun lights the Moon, and as the Moon moves around the Earth we see more or less of the bright part, so it looks different each night.
Matching: "The Sun lights up the Moon." goes to "The Moon shines." "The Moon moves around the Earth." goes to "It looks different each night."
- Why does the Moon shine? "The Moon shines because the Sun lights it up." Accept: because the Sun lights up the Moon.

### Page 5. Tell It Back (RI.1.2, RI.1.1)
Passage: "A train runs on tracks. A train has an engine and cars. The engine pulls the cars. Trains carry people and things. Trains stop at stations. People get on and off at a station."
- What is this text about? Trains.
- Write three facts: any three facts stated in the text, for example, trains run on tracks; the engine pulls the cars; trains stop at stations; people get on and off at a station. Word bank for spelling support: tracks, engine, cars, stations, people. No sentence starters. Accept any three facts from the text, in the child's own words.

## Parent guidance

These are nonfiction texts: they tell true facts, not stories. If the child is stuck, point back to the passage: every answer is in the text. On Page 1, the main topic is what the WHOLE text is about, not just one fact. On Page 2, a fact counts only if the text really says it. On Page 4, one fact makes another happen. On Page 5, let the child tell the facts back in their own words; spelling from the word bank is enough. One page a day; reread the passage together before the child writes.
