# Tens and Ones to 100 — Resource Page Notes

Grade 1 Math. Five printable worksheets that teach what the two digits
in a two-digit number mean: how many tens and how many ones.

## What children learn

- Ten single ones join to make one ten.
- Any two-digit number up to 100 can be read as tens and ones,
  including teens (like 13 and 19) and multiples of ten (like 40 and 50).
- Given a number, children can say its tens and ones, and given tens
  and ones, they can write the number.
- Two two-digit numbers are compared by their tens first, then their
  ones, and the comparison is written with >, <, or =.

## Page by page

### Page 1: Ten Ones Make One Ten

Children watch 10 single ones become 1 ten-rod, then circle groups of
10 among stars and dots and count the tens and ones. The page ends by
counting ten ten-rods to 100.

Answers: 10 ones = 1 ten; stars: 2 tens 4 ones = 24; dots: 3 tens
0 ones = 30; 10 tens = 100.

### Page 2: Count the Rods and Ones

Children count the turquoise ten-rods and gold ones cubes and write
the tens, the ones, and the whole number.

Answers: 26, 13, 40, 17.

### Page 3: Draw the Model

The task reverses: children read a number, write its tens and ones,
and draw the rods and ones themselves in separate drawing sections.

Answers: 36 is 3 tens 6 ones; 19 is 1 ten 9 ones; 50 is 5 tens 0 ones.

### Page 4: Compare with >, <, =

Children compare two numbers using base-ten models and write one
symbol in the box: > for greater than, < for less than, = for equal.

Answers: 34 < 43 (the tens decide); 52 < 57 (the ones decide);
60 = 60 (equal); 81 > 18 (the tens decide).

### Page 5: Compare and Order

Independent practice with no pictures: circle the greatest number,
compare pairs, and order three numbers from least to greatest.

Answers: greatest is 82; 71 > 17, 36 < 63, 45 = 45; least to greatest
is 19, 29, 91.

## Tips for grown-ups

- Let children count the cubes in a rod out loud: a ten is ten ones
  joined together, not just a longer block.
- When comparing, ask "which number has more tens?" first. Only when
  the tens match do the ones matter.
- The separate parent answer key lists every answer. Nothing in this
  pack goes above 100, and two-digit addition comes in a later pack.
