# Meet the Characters - Resource Page Notes

Grade 1 Reading, Story Elements. Five connected stories about two friends, Maya and Theo, so character knowledge builds page to page. The child reads every story. Progression runs from supported (circling) to independent (open lines).

## What the pack teaches

- Page 1: Name a character trait and find the action in the story that shows it.
- Page 2: Read feelings from what a character does. The feelings are never named in the text.
- Page 3: Explain why a character makes a choice, and what the choice shows about them.
- Page 4: Tell how a character feels at the start and the end of a story, and what changes how they feel.
- Page 5: Tell how a character meets a challenge, name the trait it shows, and explain how the response shows it.

Standards (internal planning only, never on a child page or the printable key): RL.1.3, RL.1.7.

## The stories

- Page 1, What Is Maya Like? A new girl, Ana, sits alone at lunch. Maya invites her to sit with them and shares her crayons.
- Page 2, How Does Theo Feel? Theo works on a puzzle. The last piece will not fit. He frowns and pushes it hard, then stops, turns the piece around, and it fits.
- Page 3, Why Does Theo Make That Choice? Maya and Theo find a red mitten. Maya wants to keep it. Theo says it belongs to someone and they should put it in the lost and found box. The next day, a boy finds his mitten.
- Page 4, How Does Maya Feel? Maya will read her story to the class. Her hands shake and she is scared. Theo tells her she can do it. She reads, her voice grows strong, the class claps, and she smiles.
- Page 5, Theo Meets a Challenge. Maya and Theo plan a picnic. Rain falls. Theo says they can have the picnic inside. They spread a blanket on the floor and eat.

## Answers

- Page 1: kind; "Maya shares her crayons with Ana."
- Page 2: frustrated (accept upset, mad); he frowns (accept he pushes the piece hard); happy (accept proud).
- Page 3: because the mitten belongs to someone (accept because it is not theirs); responsible; because he puts the mitten where its owner can find it (accept because he does the right thing with something that is not his).
- Page 4: scared (accept nervous) at the start; proud (accept happy) at the end; because she reads her story and the class claps (accept because Theo tells her she can do it, or because she does it).
- Page 5: the rain falls (accept the picnic is ruined); he says they can have the picnic inside (accept he finds a new way); flexible (accept creative) because he changes the plan and has the picnic inside (accept because he does not give up on the picnic, or because he thinks of a new way).

## For parents

- On Page 1, shy describes Ana, not Maya. The other two action choices are about Ana sitting alone and a new girl coming to class, not an action that shows Maya is kind.
- On Page 2, ask how Theo's face and hands show how he feels. The child should point to what Theo does, not just name a feeling.
- On Page 3, the trait is responsible, not honest. Theo does the right thing with something that is not his, and makes sure its owner can find it.
- On Page 5, the trait is flexible, not brave. Theo does not give up on the picnic. He changes the plan and finds a new way.
