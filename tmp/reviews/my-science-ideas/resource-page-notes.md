# My Science Ideas
## Text for the website resource page (no separate parent-guide PDF)

**Grade/subject:** Kindergarten Science
**Standards:** Michigan K-PS2-1, K-PS2-2 (pages 3-4), K-ESS2-1, K-ESS3-2, K-PS3-1/2 (pages 1-2), K-LS1-1, K-ESS3-1, K-ESS2-2, K-ESS3-3 (page 5).
**RCS units (Rochester Community Schools, Science K, 2026-27, in order):** Weather and Climate (Weeks 1-11), Motion: Pushes and Pulls (Weeks 12-25), Plants & Animals Live Here (Weeks 26-37).

**Learning objective.** This is the first explanatory Kindergarten Science pack: every page teaches the idea in big, clear words on the child page first, then the child practices it with one simple check. The existing science packs are investigations where the child does and records; this pack is the "learn the idea first" companion. Read the idea box together, then let your child do the check.

**How the pages work.**

- Page 1, What Is the Weather? The idea box says: "Weather is what the sky is doing today. It can be sunny, cloudy, rainy, snowy, or windy. Weather can change from day to day." The child draws a line from each weather picture to its word. Answers: sunny, cloudy, rainy, snowy, windy.
- Page 2, Weather Changes and Staying Safe. The idea box says: "Weather follows patterns. Some days are sunny, some days are rainy. When a storm comes, we go inside to stay safe. In snow, we dress warmly." The child counts the days on a 7-day week chart, then circles the safe choice for a storm and for snow. The chart has 4 sunny days and 3 rainy days (Mon, Tue, Thu, Sat sunny; Wed, Fri, Sun rainy). Safe choices: for a storm, go inside a building; for snow, coat, hat, mittens, and boots.
- Page 3, What Is a Push? What Is a Pull? The idea box says: "A push moves things away from you. A pull brings things closer to you." The child circles PUSH or PULL for each picture. Answers: shopping cart, PUSH; wagon, PULL; cardboard box, PUSH; sled, PULL.
- Page 4, Pushes and Pulls Change How Things Move. The idea box says: "A push or a pull can make things start, stop, go faster, go slower, or turn. A stronger push makes things go faster and farther." The child draws a line from each picture to what happened. Answers: STARTED, STOPPED, WENT FASTER, TURNED. The stopped ball against the block is the clearest picture; the started ball goes from still to rolling, the faster ball has long motion lines next to a slow ball, and the turning ball follows a curved arrow around a bend.
- Page 5, What Do Plants and Animals Need? The idea box says: "Plants and animals need food, water, air, and a home to live and grow. We can care for the Earth by using less, reusing things, and recycling." The child draws a line from each living thing to what it needs, four options per row, and some options are NOT needs. Answers: Plant needs sunlight and water (not food, plants make their own food, and not a toy). Dog needs water, food, and air (not clothes). Frog needs water (not sunlight, only plants need sunlight to make food; not a blanket; and a house is not a frog's home, a frog's home is a pond). Then the child circles the ways we care for the Earth: RECYCLE and PLANT A TREE, not LITTER.

**Talk about it.** After each page, ask "What did you learn?" and "How do you know?" The idea box is the science at this age: a child who can say the idea in their own words has learned it. If your child wants to test an idea, the investigation packs (Pushes & Pulls, Weather Watchers, Living Things, Taking Care of Our Earth) are the "try it yourself" companions to this pack.
