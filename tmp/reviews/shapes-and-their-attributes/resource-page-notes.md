# Shapes and Their Attributes: Resource Page Notes

Grade 1 Math. Five printable worksheets that teach children to
tell shapes apart by what defines them: how many sides and
corners they have, whether their sides are equal, and whether
their corners are square. Turning, growing, or recoloring a shape
does not change what kind of shape it is.

## What children learn

- A shape can turn, grow, or change color and still be the same
  kind of shape.
- Count the sides and corners to name a shape: a triangle has 3
  and 3, a pentagon 5 and 5, a hexagon 6 and 6.
- A square needs 4 equal sides and 4 square corners. A turned
  square is still a square.
- Two trapezoids make a hexagon, and two half-circles make a
  circle, when the pieces fit exactly.
- Draw a shape from a rule: pointing down, tall and skinny,
  turned like a diamond, or with 5 sides.

## Page by page

### Page 1: Same Shape, Different Look

A worked row shows three triangles that look different but are
all triangles: 3 sides, 3 corners, closed. Children circle every
triangle, then every square.

Answers, triangles: big triangle, side-pointing triangle, tall
skinny triangle, pointing-down triangle. Not the open shape: it
is not closed. Not the purple shape: it has 4 sides.

Answers, squares: square, rotated square (diamond orientation),
big square, small square. Not the rectangle: its sides are not
all equal. Not the rhombus: its corners are not square corners.

### Page 2: Count the Sides and Corners

A worked hexagon example numbers its 6 sides in order around the
shape, with a dot on each corner. Children count sides and
corners for four more shapes, then answer two mystery questions
from counts alone.

Answers: hexagon, 6 sides, 6 corners; pentagon, 5 sides,
5 corners; trapezoid, 4 sides, 4 corners; rectangle, 4 sides,
4 corners; triangle, 3 sides, 3 corners. Mystery: 5 sides and
5 corners is a pentagon. Mystery: 4 sides and 4 corners could be
any two of square, rectangle, trapezoid, rhombus.

### Page 3: Is It a Square?

Six shapes carry side ticks and square-corner marks. Children
circle the squares, then answer two reasoning questions: why a
diamond is a square, and why a rectangle is not.

Answers: circle the squares: square, diamond, big square, small
square (not the rectangle, not the rhombus). Why is it a square?
4 equal sides, 4 square corners. Why is it NOT a square? Not all
sides are equal. The corners are square.

### Page 4: Put Shapes Together

Three panels. In each, children circle the pieces that make the
target shape. The correct pieces fit exactly: the trapezoids are
the target hexagon's own halves, and the half-circles share the
target circle's size.

Answers: 1. (A) the two trapezoids. 2. (A) the two half-circles.
3. (A) the bottom trapezoid.

### Page 5: Draw It My Way

Four drawing boxes, one rule each. There is no single correct
drawing; check each one against its rule.

Rules: a triangle pointing down; a rectangle taller than it is
wide; a square turned like a diamond; a shape with 5 sides.
Count the sides together rather than correcting the drawing.

## Tips for grown-ups

- Let your child explain in their own words. Asking "How do you
  know?" builds the reasoning this pack teaches.
- If the diamond is called a different shape, turn a square
  object slowly and ask what stayed the same: 4 equal sides,
  4 square corners.
- A 5-sided shape can look many ways. Any closed shape with 5
  straight sides counts.
- The separate parent answer key lists every answer. Nothing on
  the child pages gives an answer away by position.
