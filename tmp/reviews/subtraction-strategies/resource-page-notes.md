# Subtraction Fact Strategies Within 20: Resource Page Notes

Grade 1 Math. Six printable worksheets that teach children to
subtract within 20 by choosing a strategy, not by memorizing facts
in isolation.

## What children learn

- Count back from the big number instead of starting at 1.
- Count up from the small number to find the difference, which can
  help a lot when the numbers are close together.
- First, get to 10: take away just the extra ones.
- Then take the rest: after reaching 10, take what is still left
  to take.
- Use a known addition fact to subtract.
- Different problems suit different strategies, and any strategy
  the child can show or explain that gives the correct difference
  is right.

## Page by page

### Page 1: Count Back

Children follow one worked number-line example (13 - 4 = 9: start
at the circled 13, hop back to 12, 11, 10, 9), then draw their own
backward hops for three more problems, always starting from the
big number, which is already circled.

Answers: 13 - 4 = 9; 12 - 3 = 9; 15 - 2 = 13; 11 - 4 = 7.

### Page 2: Count Up to Find the Difference

A worked example shows the whole count (12 - 8 = 4: start at the
circled 8, count up 4 hops, 9, 10, 11, 12). A dot-row comparison
asks how many more one row has than the other, then two number-line
problems start from the small number.

Answers: 12 - 8 = 4; 14 - 11 = 3; 15 - 13 = 2; 11 - 7 = 4.

### Page 3: First, Get to 10

This page teaches only the first step. A worked example shows the
Before pair (a full ten plus 3) and the After pair (the ten
standing, the 3 crossed out): 13 - 3 = 10. Three practice problems
each have a single blank and each lands on 10.

Answers: 13 - 3 = 10; 15 - 5 = 10; 12 - 2 = 10; 14 - 4 = 10.

### Page 4: Reach 10, Then Take the Rest

A numbered three-step example builds on page 3. Step 1 takes 3 to
reach 10 (13 - 3 = 10). Step 2 starts from that picture, where the
3 are already gone, and takes 1 more from the ten (10 - 1 = 9).
Step 3 shows the 9 remaining dots on their own with the full chain:
13 - 4 = 13 - 3 - 1 = 9. Two practice problems follow the same
pattern with stepped equation blanks. A plain X marks the first
take; a circled X marks the second take, so both read clearly even
in grayscale.

Answers: 13 - 4 = 9; 12 - 5 = 7; 15 - 7 = 8.

### Page 5: Use Addition to Subtract

A fact-family triangle (7 + 6 = 13, with 13 - 6 = 7 and
13 - 7 = 6) leads into two complete-the-family problems and two
think-addition problems, where the child fills in the missing
addend first and then the difference.

Answers: 13 - 6 = 7, 13 - 7 = 6; 13 - 4 = 9, 13 - 9 = 4;
14 - 6 = 8, 14 - 8 = 6; 15 - 9 = 6; 12 - 7 = 5.

### Page 6: Pick Your Strategy

Six problems. For each, the child circles one of four strategy
choices (Count Back, Count Up, Make 10, Use Addition) and solves.
The most natural fit for each problem is: 14 - 3 Count Back,
13 - 11 Count Up, 16 - 8 Make 10, 12 - 5 Make 10, 15 - 9 Use
Addition, 11 - 4 Count Back.

Answers: 14 - 3 = 11; 13 - 11 = 2; 16 - 8 = 8; 12 - 5 = 7;
15 - 9 = 6; 11 - 4 = 7.

## Tips for grown-ups

- On page 6, praise the strategy the child picked when the
  difference is right. The most natural fit is listed, not the
  only one. Any strategy the child can show or explain that gives
  the correct difference counts.
- If a child counts on their fingers, that is fine: counting back
  and counting up are strategies this pack teaches.
- Pages 3 and 4 are meant to be done in order. Page 3 practices
  only getting to 10; page 4 adds the second take.
- The separate parent answer key lists every answer. Nothing on
  the child pages names a strategy as the only correct one.
