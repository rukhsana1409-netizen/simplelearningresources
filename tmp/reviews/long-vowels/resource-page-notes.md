## How to use this pack

This pack teaches the long vowel sounds in print, in the order the
user set: final-e first, then vowel teams. The silent e makes the
vowel say its name: cap says a, cape says A. Say the vowel NAME, not
the letter names of the team: "ai says A", not "a-i". If the child is
stuck on a word, cover the team or the silent e with a finger, read
the rest of the word, then add the long sound. One page a day; reread
the Page 5 sentences together.

The ea in sea says long e. Words like bread and head, where ea says
something else, are not used in this pack. The ow in snow says long
o, not the sound in cow; no other ow words are used. No two-syllable
words, no digraph teaching, no phonemic-awareness-only tasks, no
tracing, no timed drills, no prediction.

## Page 1: Meet Final-e (RF.1.3c)

The adult models each short/long pair first, short then long: cap,
cape. Hear how the vowel changes? The child circles the long-vowel
word in each pair. The silent e in the long word is teal. Answers,
in order: cape, kite, hope, tube. Short words: cap, kit, hop, tub.

## Page 2: Read and Match: Final-e Words (RF.1.3b, c)

The child reads the word and draws a line to the picture it names.
Answers, in order: cake, bike, rope, home, tube, kite. Pictures on
the right are in mixed order, so no picture sits beside its own word.
Patterns covered: a_e (cake), i_e (bike, kite), o_e (rope, home),
u_e (tube).

## Page 3: Meet the Vowel Teams (RF.1.3c)

The adult reads each example first. The child circles the two letters
in each word that make one sound. The example words are printed
entirely in dark blue, so the child identifies the team without the
answer highlighted; only the large team heading at the top of each
card is teal. Teams: ai says A, ay says A,
ee says E, ea says E, oa says O, ow says O. Example words: rain,
play, bee, sea, boat, snow. Ay usually comes at the end, ai in the
middle. Ow in snow says long o, not the sound in cow.

## Page 4: Sort by the Long Vowel Sound (RF.1.3c, L.1.2d)

The child reads each word in the bank and writes it in the column of
its long vowel sound. This is the pack's spelling element: different
teams can make the same long sound. Answers: Long A: rain, sail,
play, day. Long E: bee, tree, sea, bean. Long O: boat, soap, snow,
blow. Check at the bottom: the child reads the sorted words back and
checks each word has its long vowel sound.

## Page 5: Read the Sentences (RF.1.3b, c; RF.1.1a light touch)

The child reads the sentence and draws a line to the picture it tells
about. Every word is decodable from taught patterns or a
high-frequency word (the, is, in, can, on, I, a). The adult points
out the capital letter at the start and the period at the end.
Answers, in order:
1. "The cat can see the bee." (cat looking at a bee)
2. "The boat is on the lake." (rowboat on a calm lake)
3. "I play in the rain." (child in a yellow raincoat splashing in a
puddle with a small red toy boat)
4. "The kite is in the tree." (large colorful kite caught in the top
branches of a simple tree)
The two boat pictures are visually distinct: the Page 3 boat is a
sailboat with a red sail on waves; the Page 5 boat is a rowboat with
oars on a calm lake. The Page 3 bee is alone; the Page 5 bee is
watched by a cat. The Page 3 rain is a cloud with drops; the Page 5
rain is a child splashing in a puddle with a toy boat. The Page 5
kite is a large colorful kite caught in a simple tree with few
leaves, its tail hanging down.

## What is new against Kindergarten and Resources 1 and 2

Kindergarten teaches single letter sounds and CVC words only; no
long vowel appears anywhere in Kindergarten. Resource 1, Sounds in
Words, was phonemic awareness by ear with no printed target words;
its long/short hearing work (cake, kite, rope, tube) is the ear
foundation this pack now puts into print. Resource 2, Word
Detectives, taught the five consonant digraphs; its adult-read meet
examples whale (wh plus a_e) and phone (ph plus o_e) already contain
the final-e pattern this pack now teaches. This pack adds final-e and
the six vowel teams ai, ay, ee, ea, oa, ow, reading one-syllable
words with those patterns, sorting words by long vowel sound, and
reading short decodable sentences. It does not teach other ea or ow
pronunciations, two-syllable words (Resource 10), or timed fluency
(Resource 9).
