## Pages and answer keys

1. **Let's Read Together** (fluency) — Sam and the cat, six decodable
   sentences; Listen → Read Together → Read Again, with a color-when-
   done circle per step. No timers or speed pressure.
2. **Read & Choose** — The pup story (sad → glad). Exactly one correct
   answer per question: Q1 box (bed/rug), Q2 sad (mad/bad), Q3 hug
   (pat/sip). Choices are word-only chips; the illustration shows Ben
   hugging the pup with NO box, so Q1 must be read. Story uses
   "The pup is glad." (user revision; "glad" is taught in context
   here — Q2's choices deliberately stay mad/bad, both decodable).
3. **Read & Complete** — The hen story; word bank pen · red · log,
   each used once: (1) pen, (2) red, (3) log. The hen illustration
   shows no pen and no log, so those answers come from the text.
4. **Read & Draw** — "A red fish is in a pond. The sun is up. A bug is
   on a rock." No illustration at all; one large blank drawing area.
   *fish, pond, rock* are beyond the taught phonics and are flagged as
   adult-supported (dotted underline + named in the adult line).
5. **Read & Sequence** — The kite story in four beats (flying → wind
   blows it into the tree → a man retrieves it with a long pole → the
   children have it back, glad). Four consistent illustrations in a
   shuffled 2 × 2 grid; children write 1–4 in the box under each.
   Answer key: top-left **3** (pole rescue), top-right **1** (flying),
   bottom-left **4** (kite back), bottom-right **2** (stuck). Untaught
   words are dotted and listed for the adult: and, fly, wind, blows,
   into, tree, has, back. No child climbs the tree; the adult rescues
   with a pole while the children watch.

## Reading-level conventions (pack-wide)

Every word a child must read is a taught common word (the 11 from
Common Words — Read & Learn), a true short-vowel CVC/VC word, a
pattern extension of a taught pattern (kite/pole via like's silent e,
by via my), a word already read earlier in this pack (glad, Page 2),
or an explicitly adult-supported word flagged with a dotted underline
and named in the adult guidance line. "Box" follows the precedent set
in Understanding Print & Sentences.
