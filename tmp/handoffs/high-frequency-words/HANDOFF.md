## What this pack is

Five pages that teach 12 high-frequency words by sounding out the regular parts and remembering only the tricky part. No whole-word memorization, no heart symbols, no phonics terminology on the child pages. A teal dot marks a regular sound-spelling correspondence; a teal underline marks the tricky part. The 12 words are split into two sets of six: Set 1 was, said, could, some, give, once; Set 2 were, who, been, know, would, any.

## The five pages (as approved)

**Page 1. Meet the Words (Set 1).** Six panels, each with the marked word, a picture, and a short phrase. Markings: was (dot w, lines a and s; the s says z, like in is), said (dot s, line ai, dot d), could (dot c, one line under oul, dot d; the ou says short oo like in book and the l is silent, one tricky unit), some (dot s, line o, dot m, final e unmarked and silent), give (dot g, line i, dot v, final e unmarked and silent), once (line o, dot n, line ce; the o makes a w sound and ce says s). Phrases: "He was a puppy.", "She said, 'Hello!'", "I could lift it.", "I have some apples.", "Give me the ball.", "I went there once."

**Page 2. Meet More Words (Set 2).** Six panels, same layout. Markings: were (dot w, line ere), who (line wh, line o; the wh says h, not w, and o says long oo), been (dot b, line ee, dot n; the ee says short i), know (line k, dot n, dots ow; the k is silent and ow says long o, taught), would (dot w, one line under oul, dot d; the same as could), any (line a, dot n, dot y; the a says short e and y says ee). Phrases: "They were at the lake.", "Who is at the gate?", "I have been to the lake.", "I know the way.", "I would like a pen.", "Do you have any pens?"

**Page 3. Spell the Words (Set 1).** Six spacious spelling activities, one per Set 1 word. Fill in the tricky part on a teal blank, then write the whole word on a ruled line. Missing parts: was a, said ai, could oul, some o, give i, once o. Each panel has the matching picture from Page 1. The other six words get their spelling practice on Pages 4 and 5.

**Page 4. Use the Words in Sentences (Set 2).** A shuffled word bank (know, any, were, would, who, been) and six sentences, each with a picture at the right. Exactly one word fits each sentence, verified: 1. "The dogs ___ on the mat." (were), 2. "___ is at the gate?" (who), 3. "I have ___ to the lake." (been), 4. "I ___ the way home." (know), 5. "I ___ like a red pen." (would), 6. "Do you have ___ cups?" (any).

**Page 5. Read and Write on Your Own.** A short, natural story (57 words, 8 sentences) about Sam, his dog Max, a red ball in the lake, and Ben helping. It uses seven target words where they fit naturally (once, was, said, could, would, some, give); the other five (were, who, been, know, any) are not forced in. Story: "Once, Sam was at the lake with his dog Max. A red ball was in the lake. Sam said, 'Max, get the ball!' Max could not get it. Sam said, 'I would like some help.' Ben was at the lake. Ben said, 'I will give you the ball.' Ben helped get the ball, and Sam was so glad." Picture: two boys, a dog, and a red ball at the lake. Below the story, a 12-word bank and two spacious writing areas (numbered, two ruled lines each) for two sentences of the child's own.

## The review corrections (her direction)

1. **v1 to v2 (markings, once phrase, Page 5 story, Parent Guide).** The teach box on Pages 1-2 overlapped the first row of panels, so it was removed; the panels now start below the two-line instruction. The s in was changed from a dot to a line (s saying z is not the regular sound of s). The once phrase changed from "I went once." to "I went there once." Page 5 was rewritten from a dialogue forcing all 12 words into a natural story using seven words, with a new picture (two boys, a dog, a red ball at the lake). Parent Guide corrected: was (a and s both tricky), once (o makes a w sound though there is no w), who (w is silent), been (some people say it like bean; spelling the same), would (same as could), and the Page 5 answer (story uses seven words, not all 12). Pages 3 and 4 unchanged.
2. **v2 to v3 (could and would markings, once explanation, Page 5 sentence).** could and would are now marked with ONE underline under "oul" (not two separate underlines for ou and l), so the silent l is included in the single tricky unit and does not imply a separate sound. The Parent Guide once explanation was replaced with her exact wording: "once sounds like 'wuns.' Its spelling is unusual, so help your child remember the tricky parts." Page 5: "Ben got the ball" changed to "Ben helped get the ball." Pages 3 and 4 unchanged.

## Parent Guide (separate one-page guide, NOT in the student bundle)

One page, for adults only. Plain language, no standards codes, no em dash. Gives the helping routine (sound out the dotted letters, point to the underlined part, say the word together), the tricky part in each of the 12 words with corrected sound-to-spelling explanations (including the silent l in could and would, wh in who, silent k in know, the unusual spelling of once, and pronunciation variation in been), all answers (Page 3 missing parts, Page 4 sentences, Page 5 story words), and a short "If your child finds it hard" section.

