## What this pack is

Five pages that teach a first grader to read poems: how a poem is arranged in lines and stanzas, how rhyming words sound the same at the end, how words in a poem tell what we see, hear, feel, and smell, and how the words tell how a poem feels. Page 5 is independent writing: the child writes a short poem about a garden picture, using the word bank or their own words, with a three-item self-check.

## The five pages (as approved)

**Page 1. Meet a Poem.** A watercolor gray cat with a red ball on a rug beside the poem "My Cat" (4 lines, 2 stanzas). The child counts the lines (4) and the stanzas (2). Then the comparison: the same words arranged two ways, in lines (A) and in one long line (B); the child circles the one written like a poem (A). A Remember box: "Poems often arrange words in lines. / A group of lines is called a stanza." Adult line below: "Point to each line of the poem. Poems arrange words in lines, often in groups called stanzas."

**Page 2. Listen for Rhymes.** A watercolor child in a yellow rain hat jumping in a puddle beside the poem "Rain" (4 lines: hat/that, play/day). The child circles the rhyming words in the poem. Two choice rows: "Which word rhymes with sun?" (run/rain, answer run) and "Which word rhymes with cake?" (lake/cat, answer lake). A Remember box: "Words that rhyme sound the same at the end." Adult line below: "Say the two words aloud. Do they sound the same at the end?"

**Page 3. Words We Can Feel.** Her poem "In the Garden" (verbatim): "A red rose grows beside a tree, / A little bee hums next to me. / Soft green grass is at my feet, / The rose smells fresh and sweet." A watercolor garden (red rose, bee, tree, grass, sun) beside the poem. Four questions, each with two word choices: See (rose/hums, answer rose), Hear (hums/sweet, answer hums), Feel (soft/red, answer soft), Smell (sweet/green, answer sweet). A Remember box: "Words can tell what we see, hear, feel, smell, and taste." Adult line below: "Point to the picture. What do you see? What do you hear?"

**Page 4. How Does the Poem Feel?** Two poems, hers verbatim. "Play Day": "The sun is bright, I run and play, / I jump and smile throughout the day. / My dog runs fast beside my feet, / We race along the sunny street." (happy; clue words run, play, jump, smile, race, sunny). "My Lost Ball": "My little ball is gone today, / I looked for it along the way. / I sit alone and give a sigh, / I miss my ball and want to cry." (sad; clue words gone, looked, alone, sigh, miss, cry). Each poem has a watercolor illustration (a happy boy running with a brown dog in the sun; a sad boy sitting alone on a step, with no ball visible, as she directed) and three feeling pills (happy/sad/calm); the child circles one and underlines one or two words that tell the feeling. A Remember box: "The words in a poem tell how it feels." Adult line below: "Look at the picture first. Then find the words that tell the feeling."

**Page 5. My Little Poem.** One large watercolor garden scene shown whole (a girl in a sunny garden with roses, a bee, and the sun). A Word Bank panel: "Words that rhyme: sun, run, day, play" / "Words that tell what it is like: soft, sweet, red, warm" / "Words that tell how I feel: happy, glad, calm". Four writing rows with ruled lines: "I see" / "I hear" / "I feel" / "I am". A self-check panel with three checkboxes: "Did I write my poem in lines?", "Did I use a word that tells what I see, hear, or feel?", "Did I use a feeling word?" No adult line on this page. Page 5 is independent application, not assessment.

3. **Page 4 poems replaced (2026-11-19, plan approval).** The draft poems were replaced with hers, verbatim: "Play Day" and "My Lost Ball". The happy/sad/calm choices are kept; the child underlines one or two supporting clues. The sad child is shown without the missing ball visible, as she directed.
4. **Page 1 final corrections (2026-11-19, visual review).** The comparison question now reads "Which one is written like a poem? Circle it." The Remember box now reads "Poems often arrange words in lines. A group of lines is called a stanza." The bottom guidance now reads "Point to each line of the poem. Poems arrange words in lines, often in groups called stanzas." The Parent Guide Page 1 explanation was updated to match, clarifying that poems can contain sentences and that sentences are not defined by appearing on one printed line. ## Builder and rebuild

The builder reads the JPEGs from its output directory (`IMG_DIR = OUT_DIR`); to rebuild from a repo checkout, copy the 6 JPEGs into the output directory first.

**Rebuild command** (from a repo checkout, repo-local venv has reportlab, pypdf, and PIL):

```
cd worksheet-generator && ../.venv/bin/python build_poetry_and_feeling_words.py
```

This regenerates the 5-page bundle, the 1-page parent guide, the review PDF, and the five per-page PDFs (split losslessly from the bundle) into the output directory. Rebuilt files must match the hashes above byte-for-byte; the approved finals are the lock, do not re-render them for the website.
