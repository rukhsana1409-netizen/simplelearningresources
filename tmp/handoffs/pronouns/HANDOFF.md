## What this pack is

Five pages that teach a first grader to use pronouns in place of names. The child matches each subject pronoun to its picture, replaces a repeated name with the short word, circles the pronoun that comes after the action word, circles the standalone possessive form, chooses the indefinite pronoun that fits the picture and sentence, and writes one sentence of their own using a pronoun. Every answer is visible in the picture or the sentence. No prepositions, no narrator-clue teaching (Resource 19 owns it), no possessive nouns re-taught (Resource 17 owns them), no joining words, determiners, or sentence types (Resource 23 owns them), no "first person" or "third person" language.

## The five pages (as approved)

**Page 1. Meet the Pronouns.** "A short word takes the place of a name." "Draw a line from each word to its picture." Teach box: "A pronoun is a short word that takes the place of a name. Mia is a name. She is a pronoun. I, you, he, she, it, we, and they are pronouns." One clean matching activity: seven pronoun pills in one single vertical list on the left (I, you, he, she, it, we, they), seven identical picture cards in one single vertical list on the right in shuffled order, with about 190pt of blank space between the lists for drawing matching lines. Cards, top to bottom: she (girl reading, "She is reading."), it (red ball, "It is red."), they (group with a watching girl, "They are playing."), I (boy pointing to himself, "I am Sam. I can jump."), we (group with the speaker inside it, "We are playing."), he (boy running, "He is running."), you (girl pointing at the reader, "You can jump too!"). No card sits in the same row as its own pill. The we/they speaker distinction is visually obvious: the word "We" sits inside a speech bubble above the middle child, who is inside the group, and the word "They" sits in a small bubble above the watching girl, who stands apart from the playing group. Remember box: "A pronoun takes the place of a name. She is one girl. He is one boy. They are more than one." Adult line below: "Name each picture together first. Say the pronoun with its picture."

**Page 2. Who Are We Talking About?** "Say the name first. Then use a short word." "Read each pair. Circle the pronoun that takes the name's place." Teach box: "Say the name first. Then use a short word. Mia is reading. She likes books." Five pictured sentence pairs, circle the pronoun: he ("Noah is running. ___ is running fast."), she ("Mia is reading. ___ likes books."), they ("Mia and Noah are playing. ___ are playing ball."), we ("Sam and I are building. ___ are building a tower."), it ("The toy car is fast. ___ is fast."). Remember box: "The pronoun takes the place of the name." Adult line below: "Cover the name in the second sentence. Ask: Who are we talking about?"

**Page 3. Me, Him, Her, Us, Them.** "Some pronouns come after the action." "Read each sentence. Circle the pronoun that comes after the action word." Teach box: "I, he, she, we, and they do the action. Me, him, her, us, and them come after the action word, or after to. I help Mom. Mom helps me. Dad reads to us." Five pictured items, two pills each (object pronoun and subject partner): me ("Mom helps ___.", me / I), him ("I see ___.", him / he), us ("Dad reads to ___.", us / we), her ("We thank ___.", her / she), them ("We call ___.", them / they). Remember box: "Me, him, her, us, and them come after the action word, or after to." Adult line below: "Act it out first. Who does it? Who does it happen to?"

**Page 4. Mine, Yours, His, Hers, Ours, Theirs.** "My goes before a thing. Mine stands alone." "Read each pair. Circle the word that stands alone." Teach box: "My goes before a thing. Mine stands alone. This is my book. The book is mine. His stays the same. This is his ball. The ball is his. Their kite means the kite belongs to Ana and Ben. Theirs means the same thing." Six items with determiner lead-ins and two standalone-form pills each, in a spacious two-column, three-row layout: mine ("This is my book. The book is ___.", mine / yours), yours ("This is your ball. The ball is ___.", yours / mine), hers ("This is Mia's book. The book is ___.", hers / his), his ("This is Sam's ball. The ball is ___.", his / hers), ours ("This is our dog. The dog is ___.", ours / theirs), theirs ("This is Ana and Ben's kite. The kite is ___.", theirs / ours). "Her" is never tested as a possessive here (Page 3 owns it as an object pronoun); the guide explains the two jobs. Remember box: "My, your, his, her, our, and their go before a thing. Mine, yours, his, hers, ours, and theirs stand alone." Adult line below: "Hold the thing. Ask: Whose is it?"

**Page 5. Someone, Anyone, Everyone, Everything.** "Some words do not name one person or thing." "Read each sentence. Circle the word that fits." Teach box: "Someone means one person, but we do not say who. Anyone means any one person. Everyone means all the people. Everything means all the things. We use anyone after not. I did not see anyone." Four pictured items, all four taught words as pills every time, in a different order, so the child must use the picture and the sentence to choose: Someone ("___ is at the door.", picture of a person at a door), Anyone ("I looked in the room. I did not see ___.", picture of a child peeking into an empty room), Everyone ("___ is singing. All the children are singing.", picture of children singing), Everything ("I put all my things away. ___ is in the bag.", picture of a bag full of things). Each item has exactly one defensible answer. Activity B, application: a picture of children playing with a ball and a bag nearby, "Write one sentence about the picture. Use a pronoun from the word bank." Word bank: "I, he, she, we, they, me, him, her" and "mine, yours, someone, anyone, everyone, something" (something is the optional extension only). One writing line. Self-check: "Did I use a pronoun?" No adult line on this page. Page 5 is independent practice, not assessment.

```
cd worksheet-generator && ../.venv/bin/python build_pronouns.py
```
