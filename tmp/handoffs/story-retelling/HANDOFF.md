# Story Retelling — Beginning, Middle & End — Handoff

Kindergarten Reading & Language (Literature / retelling). Five
worksheet pages, all user-approved and locked 2026-10-16, plus a
separate one-page parent answer key. Aligned with the Michigan
kindergarten reading standard RL.K.2 (with prompting and support,
retell familiar stories, including key details). Companion to —
and deliberately distinct from — Story Sequencing: that pack
taught ORDER with numbers; this pack teaches story PARTS
(beginning / middle / end) and telling the story back. No page in
this pack asks a child to number anything.

## The pages

1. **Beginning, Middle & End** — Mia's rainy day introduces the
   three parts: dark clouds and a closed umbrella (beginning),
   pouring rain and puddle-splashing (middle), sun and rainbow
   (end). Each panel names its part and carries two short
   read-aloud sentences; the child only listens and looks.
2. **Tell the Story** — a boy and his red cap: cap on (beginning),
   wind carries it away and he chases it (middle), cap back on,
   laughing (end). NO sentences — each panel has only its part
   name and the prompt "What happens here?", so the child retells
   in their own words.
3. **What Happened in the Middle?** — the ice-cream story told by
   its ends: empty cone at the cart (beginning), bench with a tall
   pink cone (end); the middle frame is a dashed "?". Circle the
   middle choice — the vendor scooping pink ice cream onto her
   cone. Distractors (same girl swinging, same girl on a bicycle)
   involve no ice cream. The vendor is the same older woman
   (glasses, blue apron) in both cart scenes.
4. **Retell a Short Story** — Lily and her lost teddy, read aloud
   from the boxed story panel (one line per part), then the three
   events IN ORDER below with part labels and the "What happens
   here?" prompt for the child's own retelling.
5. **My Story Retelling** — a boy and a big orange butterfly in
   bright flat art (sees it on a flower → follows it → it lands on
   his fingertip). Each part pairs its picture with a large empty
   drawing box; the child draws each part and tells the story OUT
   LOUD — no writing lines, per the page's instructions.

## Notes for integration

  to the preview the user approved for that page.
- All pages are US Letter (612×792).
- Five different stories and five different lead characters
  across the pack (Mia, the cap boy, the ice-cream girl, Lily, the
  butterfly boy) — no story or character repeats.

  uses bright flat illustration at the user's direction for the
