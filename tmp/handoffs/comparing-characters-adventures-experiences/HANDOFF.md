## What this pack is

Five pages that teach a first grader to compare and contrast what two characters do and feel in two stories. The child reads two short original stories starring Maya and Theo, matches experience pictures to the right character, circles only what happened to both children, draws a line from each thing that happened to the child it happened to, finishes comparison sentences from a picture word bank, and writes one sentence about how the two experiences are alike and one sentence about how they are different. Every answer is visible in the picture or the sentence. No retelling, no sequencing, no generic comprehension questions, no narrator work (Resource 19 owns it), no possessive nouns re-taught (Resource 17 owns them), no joining words, determiners, or sentence types (Resource 23 owns them), no pronouns as grammar (Resource 22 owns them), no shades of meaning or context-clue strategy (Resource 21 owns them), no "first person" or "third person" language, no "because".

## The five pages (as approved)

**Page 1. Two Adventures.** "Read both stories. Circle the right name under each picture." Two story panels, each with a large picture and the story in short lines. Story A, "Maya and the Big Slide": "Maya goes to the park. She sees the big slide. Maya feels scared. Ana goes down first and says, 'Come on!' Maya goes down the slide. She feels happy. 'Again!' she says." Story B, "Theo and the Shallow Pool": "Theo goes to the pool. He sees the shallow pool. Theo feels scared. Dad stands beside him and says, 'Come on! You can do it!' Theo steps into the water. Theo feels happy. 'Again!' he says." Theo's pool is shallow, Dad stands beside him reassuring him, and Theo steps into the water; there is no deep pool and no jumping in anywhere in the pack. Below the stories, four experience pictures in a row, each with Maya and Theo pills beneath it to circle: scared Maya at the slide (Maya), happy Theo in the pool (Theo), happy Maya on the slide (Maya), scared Theo at the pool (Theo). The pictures are distinguishable by setting and action (slide vs. water, park vs. pool), not just by who is in them. Remember box: "Each story is about one child. Look at the picture to tell whose story it is." Adult line below: "Read each story aloud first. Point to the picture and ask: Who is this?"

**Page 2. How Are They Alike?** "Circle only what happened to both children." Teach box: "Maya and Theo did some things the same. Some things happened to only one child." Six picture-supported choices in three rows of two, interleaved so the four shared experiences and the two differences are not grouped. Row 1: "Both felt scared." (Maya and Theo scared pictures) and "Went down a slide." (Maya happy only). Row 2: "Both tried something new." (Maya and Theo happy pictures) and "Stepped into the water." (Theo happy only). Row 3: "Both got help." (Ana help and Dad help pictures) and "Both felt happy." (Maya again and Theo again pictures). The child circles only the four that happened to both. Adult line below: "Say each one aloud. Ask: Did this happen to both?"

**Page 3. How Are They Different?** "Look at what happened in each story. Draw a line to Maya or Theo." Teach box: "Maya and Theo did some things that were not the same. Look at what happened in each picture. Draw a line to the child it happened to." Six labelled pictures in a shared middle area (3 by 2 grid, shuffled so Maya and Theo pictures interleave), each with a short experience label: "the big slide" (Maya), "Dad helped" (Theo), "went down the slide" (Maya), "the shallow pool" (Theo), "Ana helped" (Maya), "stepped into the water" (Theo). Two large target panels at the bottom, one headed by Maya's picture and name, one by Theo's picture and name, with about 40pt of open space between the bottom-row labels and the panels for drawing matching lines. The task is about what happened, not about naming the child in the picture. Adult line below: "Name what happened in each picture first. Then ask: Which character did this happen to?"

**Page 4. Alike and Different.** "Use the picture words to finish each sentence." A picture word bank of five chips, shuffled left to right so the order does not give the answers away: water (pool picture), Ana (Ana help picture), scared (Maya and Theo together, both looking scared), Dad (Dad help picture), slide (slide picture). Three sentence pairs with ruled blanks: "Maya and Theo both felt ___." (scared) "Maya went down the ___. Theo stepped into the ___." (slide / water) "___ helped Maya. ___ helped Theo." (Ana / Dad). Remember box: "Alike means the same. Different means not the same." Adult line below: "Point to the picture words first. Then read each sentence together."

**Page 5. Tell About Both.** "Write one way they are alike and one way they are different." One picture prompt of Maya and Theo together, smiling, at the top. Two large writing areas, each a light panel with a label and four ruled lines: "One way they are alike:" and "One way they are different:". Self-check at the bottom: "Did I tell one way they are alike and one way they are different?" No adult line on this page. Page 5 is independent practice, not assessment.
