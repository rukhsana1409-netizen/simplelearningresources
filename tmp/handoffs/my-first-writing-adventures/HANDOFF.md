# Handoff — My First Writing Adventures (Kindergarten)

**Pack:** My First Writing Adventures — 5 pages
**Placement (for Codex):** Kindergarten → Reading & Language →
Early Writing
**Status:** Finalized and user-approved page by page, 2026-10-14. NOT
in directory.js; not merged; not published. Muse generated the PDFs
and this handoff; Codex handles review/integration as usual.

Aligned to Michigan K ELA W.K.2 (informative writing), W.K.3
(narrative), W.K.5 (respond to questions and add details), and
L.K.1–2 (sentence formation and conventions), and to the
Kindergarten writing descriptors in RCS's public standards-based
report-card family guide. Deliberately distinct from Building
Simple Sentences: nothing here arranges, expands, fixes, or
completes given text — the child GENERATES the writing, mostly
about their own drawing, growing from single labels to connected
multi-sentence text. Phonetic / invented spelling is accepted and
welcomed throughout (stated in the adult notes).

## Pages

1. **Label My Picture** — an original watercolor park scene (sun,
   tree, slide, dog, ball, grass — all fully visible; the crop was
   chosen so no label target is clipped) with six white label chips
   (mini handwriting guides) and leader lines to each item. The
   Picture Words bank (sun · dog · ball · tree · grass · slide)
   sits below the scene as look-up support; the adult line directs
   trying their own spelling first. An optional "Tell more if you
   want" two-row writing area closes the page.
2. **Draw and Tell** — "Draw something you like to do": a large,
   genuinely blank drawing frame (nothing pre-drawn), three
   spacious handwriting rows, and an optional Starting Words strip
   (I like to… · I can… · This is…). Adult note: describe the
   drawing aloud first; invented spelling welcome.
3. **Tell What Happened** — a three-picture sequence of one event
   (the same child stacking blocks: short stack → tall tower →
   tower fallen, child laughing; order is obvious from the images
   alone). Each picture carries a First / Next / Last chip and two
   handwriting rows; no sentences are provided.
4. **Add More Details** (W.K.5) — before/after revision: a plain
   line-art house (transparent-background PNG so it blends into the
   panel; also included as its JPG source) with the printed sentence
   "The house is big.", three guiding questions on pastel chips
   (What color is the house? / Who is there? / What else can you
   see?), then a large white drawing area holding the SAME house for
   the child to enrich with invented details — no word bank — and
   two handwriting rows for improved sentences.
5. **My Little Story** — child-led story planner: a "My story is
   about:" title row, then Beginning / Middle / End sections, each
   with a generous blank drawing box, one handwriting row, and a
   small First / Next / Last reminder chip. One short sentence per
   part is plenty; labels and phrases count as success (adult line).

## Conventions

Progression of support: word bank + labels → optional starters →
time-word chips → question prompts → near-independent planning.
Handwriting guides are the Kindergarten three-line standard
(30 pt rows) throughout.
