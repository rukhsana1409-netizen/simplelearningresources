## What this pack is

Five pages that teach a first grader to find who tells a story. The child learns that words like I, my, we, and our can help show that a character in the story tells it, that he and she can be in any story, and to ask "Who is telling us what happened?" and use the narrator's own words as the evidence. Page 5 is independent practice: the child reads two different stories alone and tells who tells each one. Never use "first person" or "third person" as taught language.

## The five pages (as approved)

**Page 1. Who Is Speaking?** "Every story has someone who tells it. Look at each picture. Who tells the story?" Two examples side by side. A: a watercolor girl with dark hair in a ponytail and a teal dress (Lily) with a red ball in the park, "I see a red ball in the park." The child circles who tells it: Lily (answer: Lily). B: a watercolor boy with short dark hair, a yellow shirt, and blue shorts (Noah) looking at a red kite in a tree in the park, "Noah sees a red kite in the park." The child circles who tells it: Noah or someone outside the story (answer: someone outside the story). A Remember box: "Words like I, my, we, and our can tell you a character tells the story. / He and she can be in any story." Adult line below: "Point to the word I in A. In B, the story says Noah, so someone outside tells it."

**Page 2. I or Someone Else?** "Look for the words that tell you." Direction: "Read each sentence. Circle I or my if you see it. Then circle who tells the story." Four rows sharing two pictures. Kite pair (Lily looking up at a red kite in a tree): "I see a red kite in the tree." (answer: a character) and "She sees a red kite in the tree." (answer: someone outside). Dog pair (a brown dog running fast in the park): "My dog runs fast in the park." (answer: a character) and "He runs fast in the park." (answer: someone outside). A Remember box: "Words like I, my, we, and our can tell you a character tells the story. / He and she can be in any story." Adult line below: "Ask: Who is telling us what happened? Look for the words I, my, we, and our."

**Page 3. The Character Tells It.** "A character tells this story. Read the story. Who tells it? Circle the word that tells you." The story "My Kitten", hers as approved, verbatim, beside a watercolor kitten in the rain: "I see a kitten in the rain. / She is small and wet. / I pick her up. / I take her home. / She naps in my lap." Questions: "Who tells this story? Circle one. Lily / someone outside the story" (answer: Lily); "Circle the word that tells you." (answer: I); "What did Lily find? Circle one. a kitten / a ball" (answer: a kitten). A Remember box: "Words like I, my, we, and our can tell you a character tells the story." Adult line below: "Ask: Who is telling us what happened? Lily says I." Note: "She is small and wet" and "She naps in my lap" refer to the kitten, not the narrator; he and she can be in any story.

**Page 4. Same Event, Different Voice.** "The same event, told two ways." Directions: "Read both stories. They tell the same thing. Who tells each one? / Underline the words that tell you who tells it." Version A (portrait watercolor of Noah looking up at the red kite in the tree): "I see my kite in a tree. / I run to get my dad. / Dad gets the kite down. / I hold it fast." The child circles who tells Version A: Noah or someone outside the story (answer: Noah) and underlines I and my. Version B (landscape watercolor of Noah under the tree with the red kite): "Noah sees his kite in a tree. / He runs to get his dad. / Dad gets the kite down. / Noah holds it fast." The child circles who tells Version B (answer: someone outside the story) and underlines Noah, he, and his. Then "Do they tell the same thing? Circle one. yes / no" (answer: yes). A Remember box: "The same thing can be told in two ways. Use the words as clues." Adult line below: "Underline I and my in A, and Noah, he, and his in B."

**Page 5. Who Tells This Story?** "Read and find who tells it." Direction: "Read each story. Who tells it? Underline the words that tell you." Two distinct STORIES, not an information passage. Passage A: Lily and Spot, a watercolor of Lily in her teal dress throwing a red ball to a brown dog in the park: "I have a dog named Spot. / Spot runs fast in the park. / I throw a red ball. / Spot brings it back to me." The child circles who tells this story: Lily or someone outside the story (answer: Lily) and underlines the words that tell you (I, and me if spotted). Passage B: Zoe and the snowman, a watercolor of Zoe in a red coat placing a red hat on a snowman with a round red nose: "Zoe rolls a big snowball. / She puts a red hat on top. / She gives it a red nose. / The snowman smiles in the sun." The child circles who tells this story: Zoe or someone outside the story (answer: someone outside the story) and underlines the words that tell you (Zoe, She). A Remember box: "Words like I, my, we, and our can tell you a character tells it. Use the words as clues." No adult line on this page. Page 5 is independent practice, not assessment.

## The review corrections (her direction)

1. **Teaching refinement (2026-11-19, plan approval).** Words like I, my, we, and our CAN HELP identify a character telling the story; he and she may appear in EITHER type of narration (proof: Page 3's own story says "She is small and wet" about the kitten, yet Lily tells it). NEVER teach "If there is no I or my, someone outside tells it" as an absolute rule; that sentence appears nowhere in the pack. Children ask "Who is telling us what happened?" and use the narrator's actual words as the evidence (underlined on Pages 4 and 5). Never use "first person" or "third person" as taught language.
2. **RL.1.5 and RL.1.9 removed (2026-11-19, plan approval).** Both were in the draft plan and were removed at her direction. They are recorded as separate curriculum gaps for the later reading audit. Do NOT automatically create new packs for them.
3. **Page 5 is two distinct stories (2026-11-19, plan approval).** The draft had an information passage; she directed two distinct STORIES instead, with no adult line, as independent practice.
4. **Page 2 direction (2026-11-19, final review).** The direction now reads "Read each sentence. Circle I or my if you see it. Then circle who tells the story." It fits on one line; nothing else on the page moved.
5. **Page 4 underline instruction (2026-11-19, final review).** The underline instruction was moved up into the main directions at the top; the duplicate line below the panels was removed.
6. **Page 5 labels (2026-11-19, final review).** The two passages are clearly labeled A and B, in the same style as the Page 1 labels, matching the Parent Guide.
7. **Parent Guide Page 2 explanation (2026-11-19, final review).** The guide now treats I and my as clues in THESE examples, not an absolute rule for every story: "In these sentences, I and my are clues that a character tells the story" and the answers say "no I or my in this sentence" per row.
8. **Snowman picture regenerated once during the build.** The first version had a carrot nose and the hat already on the snowman; the final version shows Zoe placing a red hat on a snowman with a round red nose, matching the text.
9. **Boundaries (locked with the plan).** Resource 13 Meet the Characters owns who is IN the story (traits, feelings, choices), never who TELLS it; Resource 4 Read and Think owns key details and retell, no narrator work; Resource 22 (future) owns pronouns as grammar (L.1.1(d)), while Resource 19 uses I, my, he, and she only as reading clues; Kindergarten Story Elements and Story Retelling are about story parts and oral beginning-middle-end, not narrator voice.
10. **Individual PDF quality (2026-11-19).** The per-page PDFs are generated by splitting the final bundle losslessly (pypdf page copy), never from screenshots, thumbnails, or compressed previews. All five are Letter size (612x792), each renders pixel-identical to its bundle page at 400 dpi, and the illustrations are high-resolution JPEGs. Preview PNGs are generated at 300 dpi for presentation. No em dash anywhere. Pages 1 and 3 are byte-identical to the approved v1 finals; only Pages 2, 4, and 5 and the Parent Guide changed in the final correction rebuild.

## Parent Guide (separate one-page guide, NOT in the student bundle)

One page, for adults only. Plain language, no standards codes, no em dash. Gives a "How to Use This Pack" introduction, the helping routine for each page (Page 1 with the point-to-I routine; Page 2 with the "In these sentences, I and my are clues" wording; Page 3 noting She is the kitten, not the narrator; Page 4 with the underline routine; Page 5 as independent reading), what the child learns, all answers (Page 1: A: Lily (clue: I). B: someone outside the story (clue: the story says Noah). Page 2: Row 1: I, a character. Row 2: no I or my in this sentence, someone outside. Row 3: My, a character. Row 4: no I or my in this sentence, someone outside. Page 3: Lily tells it. Clue word: I. Lily found a kitten. (She is the kitten.) Page 4: Version A: Noah. Version B: someone outside the story. Underline: A: I, my. B: Noah, he, his. Same thing: yes. Page 5: Passage A: Lily tells it. Underline: I (and me, if spotted). Passage B: someone outside tells it. Underline: Zoe, She.), a "Help your child tell a story" section (3 tips: tell about their day starting with I; tell the same small event twice, once starting with I and once with their name; pause while reading together and ask whether it says I or my), and a short "If your child finds it hard" section (Page 2, Page 4, Page 5 tips).
