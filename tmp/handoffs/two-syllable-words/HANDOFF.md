## What this pack is

Four pages that teach the child to split printed two-syllable words into syllables and read them, and to count syllables in printed words using the every-syllable-has-a-vowel rule. Each syllable uses a pattern the child already knows from Resources 1-3 (short vowels; blends; digraphs sh, ch, th, wh, ph; final-e; vowel teams ai, ay, ee, ea, oa, ow as in snow), so the child decodes the word one part at a time. No new sounds are taught. This is the first pack to teach two-syllable words; Resources 1-3 are all one-syllable words, and Preschool Clap the Syllables counts syllables orally by ear only.

Difficulty progresses through the task and the independence, not through length. Page 1 is guided (compound words whose parts are familiar one-syllable words). Page 2 is guided (the vowel-sound counting rule). Page 3 moves to independent splitting of non-compound words, with one modeled example first. Page 4 is independent (reading two-syllable words inside sentences).

## The four pages (as approved)

**Page 1. Split It and Read It (guided).** Compound words built from taught patterns, shown split with a clear break. Child reads each part, then reads the whole word, with a picture for each word. Adult models the first one. Words: sunshine (sun | shine), cupcake (cup | cake), rainbow (rain | bow), snowman (snow | man). 2x2 grid, large circular pictures.

**Page 2. How Many Syllables? (guided).** Teaching box: "Every syllable has a vowel sound. Say the word slowly and listen for the vowel sounds. 'Rain' has two vowel letters, but they make one sound, so it has one syllable. 'Cake' has a silent e at the end, so it has one syllable." Eight words in a 2x4 grid, each with a small picture and a response square labeled "syllables". Child says each word slowly, listens for the vowel sounds, and writes 1 or 2. Rows alternate 1- and 2-syllable words so the layout gives no pattern away. One-syllable: rain, cake, ship, frog. Two-syllable: rabbit, pencil, basket, lemon. The activity is listening for vowel sounds; there is no circling of vowel letters anywhere on the page.

**Page 3. Split the Word (guided to independent).** Model panel first: "Try it like this:" then the complete word "rabbit" printed once, in navy bold, with a teal vertical divider drawn between "rab" and "bit" inside the word, then "Read each part, then read the whole word." The division is shown inside the complete printed word so the child sees exactly where to draw their dividing line. Then six non-compound two-syllable words, each with a picture beside it. Child draws a line between the syllables, reads each part, then reads the whole word. Words and their correct divisions: pencil (pen | cil), basket (bas | ket), lemon (lem | on, closed first syllable, short e), mitten (mit | ten), chicken (chick | en), pumpkin (pump | kin). All divisions are linguistically correct and helpful for decoding; each syllable is a closed syllable with a short vowel.

**Page 4. Read It in a Sentence (independent).** Four short decodable sentences, each with one two-syllable word printed in teal. Instruction (her exact wording): "Read each sentence. Split the green word into two syllables. Then draw a line to the matching picture." Child draws a short vertical line inside the green word to mark the split, then draws a line to the matching picture (match dots provided, spatially distinct from the sentence text). Sentences: "The rabbit hops in the sun." "Mom has a red pencil." "I see a big basket." "The kitten is in the box." Pictures are scrambled relative to the sentences.

## The two refinements (her direction at plan approval)

1. **Page 2 teaches vowel sounds, not vowel letters.** The teaching box states that every syllable has a vowel sound, with the rain and cake examples showing why counting vowel letters does not work (rain has two vowel letters but one vowel sound, one syllable; cake has a silent e, one syllable). The activity is listening for vowel sounds.
2. **Page 3 divisions are linguistically correct and helpful for decoding, with one modeled example before independent practice.** The model is rab | bit. Every division is listed above.

## The two review corrections (v1 to v2, her direction)

1. **Page 3:** The v1 model panel showed "rab | bit" as two separate parts with an arrow to the whole word "rabbit". The v2 model shows the division inside the complete printed word (rab|bit, teal divider, no arrow), so the child sees exactly where to draw the dividing line.
2. **Page 4:** The v1 instruction was "Read each sentence. Draw a line to the picture that matches." The v2 instruction is her exact wording: "Read each sentence. Split the green word into two syllables. Then draw a line to the matching picture." The green word is printed in teal at 18pt with enough room to mark the split.

Pages 1 and 2 are byte-identical between v1 and v2. The Parent Guide is unchanged from v1.

## Decodability audit (every word checked against Resources 1-3)

All child-reading words on all four pages use only taught patterns (short vowels; blends; digraphs sh, ch, th, wh, ph; final-e; vowel teams ai, ay, ee, ea, oa, ow as in snow) plus familiar high-frequency words (the, is, in, can, on, has, a, I, see, like, my, and, to, go, mom, big, red) and inflectional -s. No word contains an untaught pattern (oo, ou, oy, or, er, ar, ng, soft g). Instructions and adult lines are adult-read, as in prior packs.

## Parent Guide (separate one-page guide, NOT in the student bundle)

One page, for adults only. Plain language, no standards codes, no em dash. Gives the helping routine (say the word slowly, listen for the vowel sounds, each syllable has a vowel sound, split the word between the syllables, read each part, then read the whole word), a note about counting vowel sounds rather than vowel letters (rain and cake examples), all four pages of answers including every syllable division, and a short "If your child finds it hard" section.
