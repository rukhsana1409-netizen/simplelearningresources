## What this pack is

Six pages that teach the four U.S. coins by name, value, and true relative size, then count and add them. Real U.S. Mint coin photographs, heads side, drawn at true relative diameters: quarter 24.26 mm, nickel 21.21 mm, penny 19.05 mm, dime 17.91 mm. The dime stays the smallest picture even though it is worth more than the nickel, and the pack teaches that directly. Four coins only, no bills, no half dollars, no dollar coins. No making change. Every total stays within 100 cents.

## The six pages (as approved)

**Page 1. Meet the Coins.** Four panels, one per coin, with a large coin picture, the name, and the value: penny 1 cent, nickel 5 cents, dime 10 cents, quarter 25 cents. Below, a size-order row (smallest to biggest: dime, penny, nickel, quarter) and the teaching line "The dime is the smallest coin, but it is worth more than the nickel!"

**Page 2. Match the Coins.** Two matching sets. Set A, coin pictures to names: dime to dime, quarter to quarter, penny to penny, nickel to nickel (no straight-across pairs). Set B, coin pictures to values: nickel to 25 cents, dime to 1 cent, quarter to 10 cents, penny to 5 cents (no straight-across pairs).

**Page 3. Count the Coins.** Count groups of identical coins: pennies by 1s, nickels by 5s, dimes by 10s, quarters by 25s. Totals, verified: 3 cents, 20 cents, 30 cents, 50 cents, 100 cents.

**Page 4. Add Mixed Coins.** One worked example at the top (quarter + dime = 35 cents), then five rows adding 2 to 4 mixed coins, counting largest value to smallest, each with a "Steps:" write-in line for the count-on steps. Totals, verified: 30 cents, 11 cents, 36 cents, 16 cents, 41 cents.

**Page 5. Make the Amount.** Circle coins from a coin bank strip to make a target amount. The instruction states Way 1 and Way 2 must be different. Amounts and solutions, verified by brute force: 10 cents one way (one dime); 15 cents two ways (dime and nickel, or two nickels and five pennies); 30 cents one way (quarter and nickel); 25 cents two ways (one quarter, or dime and three nickels).

**Page 6. Money Word Problems.** Four short problems with a picture and one-line story each, answers in cents. Mia has 2 dimes and 3 pennies: 23 cents. Ben has 1 quarter and 1 nickel: 30 cents. Lily has 3 nickels: 15 cents. A ball costs 40 cents; Sam has a quarter, a dime, and a nickel: Sam has 40 cents, so Yes, Sam can buy the ball.

## Parent Guide (separate one-page guide, NOT in the student bundle)

One page, for adults only. Plain language, no standards codes, no em dash. Gives the four coin values with the size note (dime smallest, quarter biggest), how to help (say the name and value together, count from the biggest value down, handle real coins from a jar, count aloud by 5s, 10s, and 25s), the full answer key for all six pages, and a short "If your child finds it hard" section.

## Builder and rebuild

**Builder:** `worksheet-generator/build_coins_and_money.py`. It imports `draw_header` / `draw_footer` / `_ASSETS` / `draw_logo` verbatim from `build_phonics_az.py`; no locked builder was modified. Coin pictures are drawn from the 900x900 px circled masters in `assets/coins-and-money/hi/`, built by `worksheet-generator/build_coin_masters.py` from the original coin images in `assets/coins-and-money/`. The v1 coins printed soft because the shared `_img_circle` helper downsamples to 280x280 px; v2 draws the 900 px masters, so every coin embeds at 623 to 2194 PPI, above the 300 PPI print standard. The first v2 masters had a dark ring from a resize fringe; fixed by a background-colored transparent fill, an alpha-min mask, and a tighter 1.06x crop.

**Rebuild command** (from a repo checkout, repo-local venv has reportlab):

```
cd worksheet-generator && ../.venv/bin/python build_coins_and_money.py
```

This regenerates the bundle, the per-page PDFs, and the parent guide into `~/workspace/your_files/coins-and-money/`. The approved finals are the lock; do not re-render them for the website.

## Coin images

Real U.S. coin photographs, heads side, at true relative sizes. Penny: Wikimedia Commons, "US One Cent Obv.png", public domain. Nickel, dime, quarter: U.S. Mint image library, 2025 Philadelphia uncirculated obverses, classic designs (not the 2026 SemiQ redesigns). The Mint server blocks command-line fetches, so these three were downloaded through the live browser. No AI-generated coins; the portraits and lettering must stay accurate.
