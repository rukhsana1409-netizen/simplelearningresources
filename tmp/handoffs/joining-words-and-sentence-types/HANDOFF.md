## What this pack is

Five pages that teach a first grader to join ideas into bigger sentences and to choose the little words that go before nouns. The child learns what and, but, and or mean and circles the joining word; learns so and because as a pair, with the same situation shown both ways; chooses a, an, the, this, that, these, and those, including a first-mention / second-mention pair that teaches why the second dog is "the dog"; names the four kinds of sentences and puts the right end mark; then joins two ideas, chooses a little word, and writes a question and a statement of their own about a kite picture. Every answer is visible in the sentences or pictures. No prediction, no inference beyond the text, no pronouns (Resource 22 owns pronouns), no vocabulary teaching (Resource 21 owns shades of meaning). The word "because" appears only on Page 2 and in the guide's Page 2 sections, where it is the taught content. The word "so" appears only as a joining word, never as an intensifier.

## The five pages (as approved)

**Page 1. Joining with And, But, Or.** Teach box first: "A joining word joins two ideas." Three models: and, "I like cats and I like dogs." (Both things are true.); but, "I like rain, but I do not like thunder." (The second idea is different.); or, "Do you want an apple or a banana?" (Pick one.). Then four circle-one items, each with a watercolor picture except the last: "I like to run ___ I like to jump." (and / but; answer: and); "I like ice cream, ___ I do not like the cold." (and / but; answer: but); "Do you want milk ___ juice?" (and / or; answer: or); "The sun is out ___ it is warm." (and / but; answer: and). A Remember box: "and joins two things together. but shows a difference. or lets you pick one." Adult line below: "Say each sentence aloud. Ask: Does it join, show a difference, or let you pick?"

**Page 2. So and Because.** Teach box first, with a watercolor of a dog in the rain: "The dog is wet because it rained." (because tells why.) and "It rained, so the dog is wet." (so tells what happens next.). Then four circle-one items: "The sun is hot, ___ we drink water." (because / so; answer: so); "We drink water ___ we are thirsty." (so / because; answer: because); "It is dark, ___ we turn on the light." (so / because; answer: so); "We turn on the light ___ it is dark." (because / so; answer: because). Items 3 and 4 are the same dark-room situation both ways, with the same picture reused; that reversal IS the teaching, not a trick. A Remember box: "because tells why. so tells what happens next." Adult line below: "Talk about the picture first. Ask: What happened first? What happened next?"

**Page 3. Little Words Before Nouns.** Teach box first: "A little word comes before a naming word." Models: "a cat" (one cat, any cat); "an apple" (an comes before a, e, i, o, u sounds); "the sun" (the one we know); "this apple" (in my hand); "that apple" (on the shelf); "these apples" (in my hands); "those apples" (on the shelf). Activity 1, a or an, four small watercolor pictures with circle-one pills: "___ apple" (an), "___ ball" (a), "___ egg" (an), "___ umbrella" (an). Activity 2, a or the, with the hint line "a introduces the dog. the means the same dog again." and three rows: "I see ___ sun in the sky." (a / the; answer: the); "I see ___ dog in the park." (a / the; answer: a, first mention); "___ dog is big." (a / the; answer: the, second mention, the same dog again). Activity 3, this, that, these, those, with two watercolor pictures: a boy holding one big apple in his hands with one bright red apple on a far shelf ("___ apple is in my hand." this; "___ apple is on the shelf." that) and a girl holding two big apples with two bright red apples on a far shelf ("___ apples are in my hands." these; "___ apples are on the shelf." those). The near apples are clearly larger and in the children's hands; the far apples are clearly visible on the distant shelves but plainly smaller. A Remember box: "a and an mean one. the means the one we know. this and these are near. that and those are far." Adult line below: "Point to the picture. Ask: Is it near or far? Is it one or more than one?"

**Page 4. Four Kinds of Sentences.** Teach box first, with a watercolor cat: Statement, "The cat is soft." (tells something.); Question, "Is the cat soft?" (asks something.); Command, "Pet the cat." (tells someone to do something.); Exclamation, "What a soft cat!" (shows a big feeling.). Activity 1, "What does it do? Circle the kind." (kind names shuffled per item): "The sun is out." (statement); "Can we play?" (question); "Put on your shoes." (command); "What a big cake!" (exclamation). Activity 2, "Put the right mark. Write . or ? or !": "The dog is big ___" (.); "Where is my ball ___" (?); "Put the book away ___" (.); "What a beautiful rainbow ___" (!). Commands are taught by what they do, not by their mark; no command with an exclamation mark is tested anywhere. A Remember box: "A statement tells. A question asks. A command tells someone to do. An exclamation shows a big feeling." Adult line below: "Cover the mark. Ask: What does this sentence do?"

**Page 5. Build and Write Sentences.** Activity 1, "Join the ideas." Pick the joiner from a choice, write the joined sentence on two generous lines each: "It is cold. We wear coats." (and / so; answer: "It is cold, so we wear coats."); "I like to swim. I do not like cold water." (but / so; answer: "I like to swim, but I do not like cold water."). Activity 2, "Choose the little word." (picture-supported): "I see ___ sun in the sky." (a / the; answer: the); "I want ___ apple for lunch." (an / a; answer: an). Activity 3, "Write your own." with a watercolor kite picture: "Write a question about this picture." (two writing lines) and "Now write a statement about it." (one writing line). A "Check your work" self-check with three tick boxes, each mapping to one activity and none implying every sentence needs a joiner and a determiner: "Did I join two ideas with a joining word?" / "Did I choose the right little word?" / "Did I write a question about the picture?" No adult line on this page. Page 5 is independent practice, not assessment.
